Librariarr provides a rich browsing experience for movies, series, and music with extensive filtering, multiple view modes, and detailed metadata panels.

## Global Search

The **Search library** field in the sidebar (or <kbd>⌘K</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl+K</kbd> from anywhere) opens a quick title search across your entire library. Results are grouped into Movies, Series, Artists, and Albums; selecting one jumps straight to its detail page. For filter-based queries and bulk actions, the palette links to the [Query Builder](/docs/features/query/).

![The global search palette with results grouped into movies, series, artists, and albums](/screenshots/search.webp)

## Library Views

### Movies

Browse all movies with poster artwork, metadata, and quality information.

### Series

Series are grouped by show identity, not just by title. Drill down into:

- **Seasons** — View seasons within a series with episode counts
- **Episodes** — Individual episodes with per-episode metadata and quality info

Anywhere an episode is shown on its own, outside its show's pages, it is named by its show and its season and episode number, for example *Breaking Bad S01E01*. This applies on the All Episodes grid, the History page, [Query](/docs/features/query/) results, hover cards and detail panels. The episode's own title appears as extra detail. An episode that has no season or episode number is shown as its show followed by its own title. The All Episodes list is ordered the same way by default: by show, then season and episode.

Each of these pages also carries a [watch history section](#watch-history-on-detail-pages) listing the individual plays for whatever you are looking at.

Two different shows that happen to share a title — *The Office* (UK) and *The Office* (US), or the 1978 and 2004 *Battlestar Galactica* — appear as **separate rows**, each with its own seasons, episodes, and watch history. Shows are matched by their series-level TVDB id (TMDB as a fallback, and the show title only when neither id is present), so the same show that exists on more than one server still shows up as a **single** merged row.

### Music

Music is organized by artist. Browse artists, albums, and individual tracks with audio format, quality, and duration details.

<Aside type="note" title="Collections are not library items">
Plex collections, Jellyfin/Emby box sets, and playlists are *containers* — they group media but have no file, resolution, or size of their own. Librariarr's sync skips them, so they never show up in your movie, series, or music listings, and a [lifecycle rule](/docs/features/lifecycle-rules/) can never match one. This includes the collections Librariarr creates itself for [collection sync](/docs/features/lifecycle-rules/#plex-collections).

If a collection was listed as an item before, the next full sync of that library removes it — no action needed on your part.
</Aside>

## View Modes

Toggle between two view modes:

| Mode | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Table** | Detailed rows with columns for title, year, resolution, codec, audio, file size, play count, and more. Columns are resizable via drag-to-resize. |
| **Card** | Poster cards in a responsive grid. Three sizes (S, M, L), plus a control for which details each card shows. |

Both views use smooth scrolling, so libraries of any size load and render without pagination. On desktop, the toolbar — search, sort, view controls, and filters — stays pinned to the top of the page while you scroll; on mobile it scrolls with the page to leave more room for content.

## Alphabet Navigation

A clickable alphabet bar (A–Z plus `#` for non-alphabetic titles) lets you jump directly to a letter. The bar highlights which letters have matching items.

### Where Each Filter Applies

- **Audio Tracks** and **Subtitle Tracks** (track-count filters) appear on **Movies** only.
- **Episode Count**, **Watched Episodes**, **Watched %** and **Last Episode Aired** (show-level filters) appear on the **Series** tab only.
- On the **Series** and **All Seasons** tabs, a filter on an episode's own property (resolution, codec, genre, year and so on) keeps a show or season when **any** of its episodes matches. The totals shown stay those of the whole show or season. Last Played, Added Date and Watchlisted judge the show or season as a whole — its most recent play and newest episode — even when combined with other filters.
- **Resolution** matches the label on the quality chip on every library page, so a film or show stored at 576 lines counts as 480P. A file with no resolution recorded shows no chip on a movie, episode or track card and is not **Other** there; on the **Series** and **All Seasons** tabs, where it counts toward a show's **Other** chip, it is.
- The **Albums** tab filters exactly like the **Artists** tab: an album is kept when any of its tracks matches, and its totals count the matching tracks.

### Multi-Select Behavior

When multiple values are selected within a single filter (e.g., Resolution: 4K and 1080P), they're combined with **OR** logic — items matching either value are shown. **Genre** is the exception: an item must carry every selected genre. When multiple different filters are active, they combine with **AND** logic.

## Detail Panel

Click any item to open a slide-out detail panel showing:

- **Poster and artwork**
- **Summary/description**
- **Full metadata**: title, year, ratings (critic, audience, user), directors, writers, cast, studio, genres, countries
- **Video details**: codec, resolution, bit depth, profile, frame rate, color info, aspect ratio, scan type
- **Audio details**: codec, channels, profile, bit rate, sampling rate, languages (for each audio stream)
- **Subtitle streams**: language, format, forced/default flags
- **File info**: size, container, path, duration
- **Stream list**: All video, audio, and subtitle streams with detailed info

The panel is the same wherever it opens — the History page, [Query](/docs/features/query/) results, the [Stream Manager](/docs/features/stream-manager/)'s session cards (with the stream's playback details on top), the rule editor's preview, and the lifecycle **Matches**, **Pending** and **Exceptions** pages. It always loads the item's current details, so it never shows an older copy of them from the list. A row that stands for a whole show or artist (a series match, a pending series action, an excepted show) opens as that show or artist, with its episode or track count and total size, and **Open full page** goes to the show's or artist's page. Where a lifecycle rule set matched the item (the rule editor's preview and **Matches**), the panel lists the matched criteria and the rule set's **Logic Preview** with those rules highlighted. Its width, set by dragging its left edge (double-click to reset), is remembered in this browser and shared by every page.

### Actions from Detail Panel

Depending on configuration:

- **Open in *server name*** — Direct link to the item on your Plex, Jellyfin or Emby server
- **Open in Radarr/Sonarr/Lidarr** — Link to the item in your *arr instance (requires integration)
- **Open in Seerr** — Link to the item's request page in Seerr (requires integration)

## Multi-Server Deduplication

When browsing with multiple servers connected:

- Items are **deduplicated** by default — only the preferred copy is shown
- An indicator shows which servers have each item
- Use the server dropdown in the library toolbar to filter by a specific server

<Aside>
  Duplicates are detected using external IDs when available — TMDB or IMDB for a movie, the series' TVDB id plus season and episode number for an episode — falling back to title and year (movies), show title plus season and episode (episodes), or artist and title (tracks). In rare cases, different items with identical metadata may be incorrectly grouped.
</Aside>

## Watch History

View detailed, per-play watch history across all your media servers. Each row represents an individual play event, showing who watched what, when, and on which device.

### Watch History on Detail Pages

Every detail page shows its own watch history, so you can see who has been watching something without leaving it for the main history page:

| Page | Shows |
|-|-|
| **Series** | Every play of every episode in the series, newest first — with the user, the date and time, and which episode |
| **Season** | The same, narrowed to episodes in that season |
| **Episode** | Every play of that one episode — the episode it belongs to, the user, the date and time, and the device — shown as the **Watch History** card beside the file and playback details |
| **Movie** | Every play of that film — the user, the date and time, and the device — as its **Watch History** card |
| **Track** | Every play of that track, as its **Listen History** card |
| **Detail side panel** | The same per-play list for whichever item you opened from a library or lifecycle list, without leaving the list |

**Every user on the server is included** — not just the account Librariarr connects with. Plays are read from the server's full, per-user history, so anyone in your household who watched an episode appears by name.

On the series and season pages every row names the episode that was played and links to it; on an episode's own page the name is plain text since you are already there. On a movie or track page the rows omit the title entirely — every play is of the thing you are looking at — and lead with the user instead. Each row also names the device or platform where available. Every one of these lists shows **five plays per page**, so a heavily-watched item stays one card rather than a long scroll. The controls beneath it step a page at a time, jump to the first or last page, and take a page number typed straight into the box — the same controls the main history page uses.

When a show exists on more than one server, plays from *every* server are listed and each row is tagged with its server name. A play is a real event on the copy that was actually played, so nothing is hidden by [deduplication](#multi-server-deduplication). The series is matched the same way the library groups shows — by series identity (its TVDB/TMDB id, falling back to the title) — so a server that spells the title differently still contributes its plays, and two different shows that share a title never blend their history.

Movie and track pages — and the detail side panel opened from any library or lifecycle list — previously showed a per-user total read live from the server, with no dates and no playback detail; they now show the same per-play list everything else does. These sections read the same stored watch history as the main history page, so they fill in after a watch-history sync (see below). The same [per-server limits](#data-sources-by-server-type) apply: on Jellyfin and Emby only the most recent play of an item carries a timestamp, so repeat plays are listed as **Unknown date** and sorted to the bottom. A server whose [watch-history source](#watch-history-sources) is Tracearr has no such limit, and its rows additionally show how far through the play got, which player was used, and whether it was transcoded.

### Syncing Watch History

Watch history data is synced from your media servers in three ways:

- **Automatic** — During each scheduled library sync, detailed play events are fetched and stored
- **On-demand** — Click the **Refresh** button on the history page to sync immediately
- **Real-time** — With [real-time sync](/docs/features/real-time-sync/) on, a play finishing on Plex triggers a refresh that fetches **only the plays since the last one stored** and appends them, so a finished episode costs one small request rather than a re-import of the whole history. Jellyfin and Emby cannot filter their history by date, so their real-time refresh re-reads the full set like the scheduled sync does.

The **Refresh** button streams live progress rather than spinning, with a phase for each server it is syncing. How that phase reads depends on the server's [watch-history source](#watch-history-sources): a **native** server shows a true percentage, because the server hands over its whole history in one response and the total is therefore known before the first row is written, while a [Tracearr](/docs/integrations/tracearr/) server shows a running count of plays imported instead — its history API is paginated by cursor and reports no total at any point, so Librariarr shows what it has actually imported rather than inventing a percentage. Leaving the page ends the sync early; a Tracearr import resumes from where it stopped on the next run.

### Watch History Sources

Every server reads its plays from one of two sources, chosen per server under **Settings → Media Servers → Watch history source**:

| Aspect | Native server history (default) | [Tracearr](/docs/integrations/tracearr/) |
|-|-|-|
| Where plays come from | The server's own history API | A Tracearr instance monitoring that server |
| Per-play timestamps | Plex only — Jellyfin and Emby report the most recent play of an item and nothing before it | Every play, on all three server types |
| Sync behaviour | Full refresh on the scheduled sync and on **Refresh** — existing records are replaced with the server's current data; the real-time refresh after a play on Plex appends only the new plays | Incremental — new and changed plays are appended, resuming from a watermark |
| Extra detail per play | Device and platform (Plex only) | Completion %, direct play vs. transcode, player, product, resolution, bitrate, source and stream codecs, subtitle decision |
| Partial plays | Not reported at all | Stored and shown, flagged as unwatched |

The native source needs no setup and is what a server uses until you map it. Using Tracearr instead needs a [Tracearr instance](/docs/integrations/tracearr/) and the per-server source switch; switching in either direction clears that server's stored history once — the two sources produce incompatible rows — and the next sync repopulates it.

### Data Sources by Server Type

What the **native** source can report differs by server type:

| Data | Plex | Jellyfin | Emby |
|-|-|-|-|
| Username | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Watched date | Yes (per play) | Most recent play only | Most recent play only |
| Device name | Yes | No | No |
| Platform | Yes | No | No |
| Play count (per item) | Yes | Yes | Yes |

<Aside type="note">
  Of the three, Plex provides the most detailed native watch history, with individual timestamps and device information for each play event. Jellyfin and Emby only report how many times each user played an item and the date of the most recent play — earlier plays are listed with no date. [Tracearr](/docs/integrations/tracearr/) removes this difference: it records plays itself as they happen, so a Jellyfin or Emby server linked to it gets full per-play history too.
</Aside>

### Play Count vs. Watch History

The **Play Count** and **Last Played** fields shown on media items in the library views are built from two sources:

- **The server's native item metadata**, read during library sync. This is scoped to the account Librariarr connects with — on Plex it is that account's own views across every server it has access to, so a brand-new Plex server may show play counts from plays that happened elsewhere under the same account.
- **The Watch History table**, which covers plays by *every* user on the server. After each watch-history sync, these plays are rolled back into Play Count and Last Played.

The two are combined by taking the higher play count and the more recent date, so a play by anyone in your household is reflected — never overwritten by the connected account's older view. This matters for lifecycle rules: criteria such as **Last Played** and **Series Last Played** read these fields, so a series someone else is actively watching will not look untouched. Watch History records themselves only include plays that occurred on that specific server.

**Partial plays do not count.** A server's native history has no notion of one, but [Tracearr](/docs/integrations/tracearr/) reports how far through each play got and marks it watched only once it crosses its completion threshold (85% by default). Plays short of that are stored and listed in the watch-history views, but they are excluded from Play Count and never advance Last Played — someone sampling four minutes of a film is not a viewing, and because both fields only ever move forward, letting one through would mark the item recently watched for good and quietly disarm every rule that reads it. An item with plays in its history but a Play Count of zero was started and never finished.

### Columns

The history table supports column visibility controls with columns grouped into categories:

| Category | Columns |
|-|-|
| Core | Title, Type, Year, Server |
| Playback | User, Watched At |
| Video | Resolution, HDR, Video Codec |
| Audio | Audio |
| File | Duration, Size |
| Device | Device, Platform (Plex only) |
| Stream | Transcode, Completion, Player, Stream Resolution |

Every column sorts, HDR, the codecs and Server (by server name) included.

The **Stream** columns default to **hidden**. They are only populated for a server whose [watch-history source](#watch-history-sources) is Tracearr, so on a native-history setup they would be an empty block of columns — turn them on from the column controls once a server is linked. **Stream Resolution** is what was actually delivered to the client and is a separate column from **Resolution**, which is the file's own; the two disagree on any transcoded play.
